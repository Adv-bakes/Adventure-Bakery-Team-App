## Purpose

To clean and sanitize the Kook-E-King cookie depositor after production so it is safe and ready for
the next day's run.

## Scope

The depositor and its food-contact parts. **Hopper, the two feed rollers, the die, and the scrapers**
come off and are washed in the sink. The **cut-off wire and its support fingers stay on the machine**
— it isn't practical to take the wire off, so they are **wiped clean in place**. Cleaning is done at
the **end of every production day** the machine is used, and a full clean again **between products
when the allergens differ**.

Operating the depositor is **SOP-502**. Lubrication and repairs are Maintenance, not sanitation.

## Responsibility

- **Sanitation / Production staff** — perform the cleaning and record it on **FRM-910**.
- **Supervisor** — checks the machine before the next run and signs the release on **FRM-910**.

> **Why a dedicated form.** The plant sanitation forms — FRM-901 Master Sanitation Schedule and
> FRM-902 Sanitation Verification Log — are Word-document attachments, not fillable in the app, and
> FRM-903 (GMP Pre-Operation Inspection) is scoped to the glass dial cover / MIG thermometer check.
> So the depositor clean and its pre-use release are recorded on **FRM-910**, a single fillable form
> (the same pattern as the mixer's FRM-909). The depositor should still be listed on the **FRM-901
> Master Sanitation Schedule** for its cleaning frequency.

## Procedure

### Two rules before you start

1. **Stop with the wire clear of the die** (furthest-back position), then **unplug the machine.**
2. **Never hose or pressure-wash the machine.** Damp cloth only on the cabinet and the depositing
   head. Water in the control panel or the motors will destroy it — the manufacturer's own warning.

### 1. Break it down

1. Swing the side guard open and remove the gear guard.
2. Loosen the hopper knobs, release the hopper hooks, and lift the **hopper** straight up off the head.
3. Remove the **feed rollers**; scrape the dough out of the roller grooves with the roller scraper
   (don't remove the gears).
4. Support the **die**, loosen the six thumbscrews, and let it drop out below.
5. Remove the **scrapers**.
6. Save reusable dough from between the rollers and die for the next run before it goes to cleaning.

The **hopper, feed rollers, die and scrapers** go to the **sink** (section 3). The **cut-off wire and
support fingers stay on** — leave them in place and wipe them clean (section 4).

### 2. The machine (wipe only)

1. Clear dough fragments from the **die slot** and the inside of the depositing head where the rollers
   sit — this is where product hangs up.
2. **Dry wipe** the head interior, top of the cabinet, and the table belts to get loose dough off.
3. **Damp wipe** the same surfaces with detergent, then wipe clean with a second damp cloth and let
   air dry. **No excessive water; nothing on the control panel.**

### 3. Wash the removable parts — in the sink

The **hopper, feed rollers, die and scrapers** are washed in the sink: **detergent → rinse →
Noble Sani-512 at 1:512 → drying rack** to air dry — 1 fl oz of Sani-512 per 4 gallons of
water (0.25 oz per gallon), about **200 ppm quat**. Sani-512 is **no-rinse** at this dilution:
don't rinse it off, and don't towel them dry; either one undoes the sanitizing. Store them
clean, dry and protected.

Check the sanitizer strength with a test strip **before you wash** — the target is about
**200 ppm quat** — and record the reading on **FRM-910**. If the strip reads below target,
remake the solution; don't wash in weak sanitizer.

### 4. The cut-off wire and fingers — wipe in place + foreign-matter check

The wire and its support fingers **stay on the machine** — it isn't practical to take the wire off.
Wipe them clean in place; **don't run them through the sink.** The wire is thin steel that can break,
so while wiping:

- Check the wire is **whole — not frayed, kinked or missing a section**, and that there is still
  **one support finger seated in each die slot**.
- If any piece of wire is missing, **find it before the machine runs again** and hold any product that
  could be affected (see SOP-502 §4). A broken wire is metal in the product.
- If the wire is frayed or damaged, fit a fresh assembly (Maintenance) before the next run.

### 5. Changing between products with different allergens

Follow **SOP-204 Allergen Cleaning Procedure** — it governs allergen changeover on all shared
equipment, including this depositor.

On top of the clean above, check **every food-contact surface under good light** before the next
product: the die holes, the roller grooves, the hopper, the wire and fingers, and the scrapers.
Anything you can see, re-clean.

### 6. Reassemble and release

1. Reinstall the die (finger-tight only) in a **clean** slot and the feed rollers (**food-safe oil**
   on the bushings — see SOP-502), refit the hopper, confirm a **support finger is seated in each die
   slot**, and return **both guards to their locked positions**.
2. The **Supervisor** checks and signs the release on **FRM-910**:
   - Hopper, rollers, die and scrapers clean and dry; wire and fingers wiped clean
   - Wire whole and accounted for
   - Machine wiped down; nothing left on it; guards locked
   - Sanitizer strength recorded

**The depositor isn't ready for production until FRM-910 is signed.**
