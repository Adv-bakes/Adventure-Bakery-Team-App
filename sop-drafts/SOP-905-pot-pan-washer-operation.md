*Machine: Douglas Machines pot, pan & utensil washer — a hot-water sanitizing warewasher that runs a
wash → rinse → dwell cycle. It both **cleans** (≈150 °F detergent wash) and **sanitizes** (≈190 °F
hot-water final rinse).*

---

## Purpose

To run the pot & pan washer so pots, pans, bowls, utensils, and sheet/baking pans come out **clean and
sanitized** and safe for food use.

The **190 °F hot-water rinse is the sanitizing step** — the ware is not sanitized unless that rinse
reaches temperature. So the rules below center on hitting and verifying the wash and rinse
temperatures.

## Scope

Warewashing of food-contact items on the pot & pan washer, and the **end-of-day cleaning of the washer
itself**.

Installation, gas/electrical work, pump lubrication, PC-board programming, spray-jet/heater/probe
replacement, and repairs are **Maintenance** — not operators. **Operators do not open the electrical
panel** (it is a live/hot panel).

## Responsibility

- **Operators** — run the washer as written, check and record the wash/rinse temperatures, and do the
  end-of-day washer clean.
- **Supervisor** — verifies temperatures are being hit and recorded; takes the washer out of service
  on a fault.
- **Maintenance** — electrical, heaters, pump, spray jets, probe, lime/scale descale, and repairs,
  with power disconnected.

## Procedure

### Before you start

- The washer is **filled to the overflow**, the **door closes properly**, and the filter baskets are
  in place.
- **Detergent** is a **non-foaming, non-caustic, aluminum-safe** warewash detergent; the automatic
  detergent feeder is **on and filled** (this machine must run with an automatic detergent feeder).
- If a **chemical sanitizer feeder** is fitted, it is **on, filled, and delivering** at the labeled
  concentration.
- **Hot surfaces / hot water:** the rinse water and steam are ~190 °F — keep hands clear during the
  rinse and dwell; the machine will not restart during the one-minute dwell.

Anything fails, or the ware isn't coming clean → don't rely on it; tell the Supervisor.

### 1. Bring it up to temperature

Set / confirm the thermostats: **WASH ≈150 °F** and **RINSE ≈190 °F**, and allow **30–60 minutes** to
heat up. Read the **temperature gauges** (not the knobs) — the gauge is what counts.

**Check and record the wash and rinse temperatures before the first load** (and periodically through
the shift). **Do not wash until both are at temperature** — below 190 °F on the rinse, the ware is not
being sanitized.

### 2. Load

- Load items **facing the wash arms — open end down** so water reaches every surface. Weight down light
  plastic items with the hold-down rack.
- **Sheet pans and baking pans** go in the correct pan rack/insert (open face toward the wash hub /
  arms). Don't stack or nest items so they shield each other.
- Don't overload or block the spray jets.

### 3. Run the cycle

Pick the cycle: **short (4 min)**, **medium (6 min)**, or **long (8 min)** for the soil level. Let it
run the **full wash → rinse → dwell** cycle — **three beeps** signal it's done. Opening the door or
pressing stop **aborts** the cycle; a restart begins again from the wash, it does not resume.

### 4. Unload and check

Ware should come out **clean**. Anything still soiled → **rescrape and rewash** (don't return dirty
ware to service). Let items **air dry**; handle sanitized ware so it stays clean.

### 5. End-of-day — clean the washer

**Turn off / disconnect power first.** Then:

1. **Drain** the machine. Spray out the wash cabinet, directing debris into the filter baskets.
2. **Remove and clean the filter baskets**; clean the wash-tank reservoir, flushing debris to the
   drain.
3. **Clean the low-water probe** metal tip (scour off scale/residue) and, if the wash tank has
   electric heater coils, **clean the coils**.
4. Put the **filters back**. **Inspect the spray jets** — clear any obstruction; report missing/worn
   jets to Maintenance.
5. **Wipe the outside** with a stainless cleaner or mild detergent. **Close the drain valve.** Leave
   it drained until the next wash.

## If something goes wrong

| Problem / display | What to do |
|---|---|
| Not cleaning | Check detergent level, wash temperature, water level, clogged jets, filters clear. |
| Rinse not hot enough | Check the rinse thermostat/gauge (target ~190 °F) and that the heat source is engaged; **hold ware — it isn't sanitized.** Persists → Maintenance. |
| Low wash pressure | Filters clear and in place, jets/end-caps in place, filled to level, excess foam (wrong detergent). |
| Not rinsing | Door closed; incoming water pressure; rinse valve — get Maintenance. |
| **Er01** fill time exceeded / low water pressure · **Er03** water loss / drain open · **Er05** not enough water in tank · **Er06** door not closed · **Er07** pump overload tripped | Address the cause (door, water, drain); reset per the panel. If it persists or shows **Er04** (control board), get Maintenance. |
| Anything inside the electrical panel | **Operators don't open it** — Maintenance only, power disconnected. |
