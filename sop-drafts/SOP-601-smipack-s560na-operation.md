*Machine: Smipack S560NA — L-seal hood shrink packer (heated seal blade + shrink chamber in one cycle).*

---

## Purpose

To run the Smipack S560NA safely and produce a clean, well-sealed shrink pack.

Two things drive the rules below. The machine has a **heated sealing blade and a hot shrink chamber**
— they burn, and film left on them is a **fire risk**. And it puts **film around the product**, so
loose film scraps and blade debris are a **foreign-matter** concern kept off the product.

## Scope

Production / packaging staff loading, running, and unloading the S560NA to shrink-wrap finished
product.

Blade replacement, coolant top-up, temperature calibration, and repairs are Maintenance — not
operators. Clearing film off the blade and out of the chamber is part of running the machine (see
sections 3–4); there is no separate cleaning SOP for this equipment (see *Cleaning* below).

## Responsibility

- **Operators** — set up and run as written; stop and report bad seals, film on hot parts, or faults.
- **Supervisor** — trains and signs off operators; takes the machine out of service on a fault.
- **Maintenance** — blade, coolant, temperature settings, electrical, and repair, with the power off
  and the machine cool.

## Procedure

### Before you start

**Don't operate this machine unless the Supervisor has trained and signed you off.**

Check:

- The **sealing blade and shrink chamber are clear of film scraps** (a fire risk and a foreign-matter
  source) and the netting is clean
- The **film reel** is the **approved food-grade film** (this film touches the baked product directly), loaded
  and tracking correctly, and undamaged
- The **emergency stop** works and returns; guards/covers are in place
- The coolant level is between **min and max** on the sight viewer
- **Never load** flammable, explosive, aerosol, or loose-powder/volatile products — they must not go
  through the machine

Anything fails → don't run it. Tag it and tell the Supervisor.

**Get yourself ready:** sleeves down and secured, no dangling ties or lanyards, hair in a hairnet.
**Keep hands clear of the sealing blade and out of the shrink chamber** — both are hot.

### 1. Set the machine

From the batch/settings chart for the product, set on the Flextron panel:

- **Sealing temperature** (blade) — enough for a clean seal, no more
- **Shrink chamber temperature** and **shrink time** — enough to shrink the film tight without
  scorching or holing it
- **Bell pressure**, **hood opening delay**, and **discharge (conveyor) speed** as charted

Let the machine reach its set temperatures before running product. Record the settings on the chart.

### 2. Make a pack

1. Feed the film so it's ready at the packaging plate.
2. Place the product on the netting, leaving **1–2 cm of space from the sealing blade**.
3. The bell lowers automatically and the blade makes the L-seal; the pack passes into the chamber and
   shrinks, then discharges on the conveyor.
4. Take finished packs off the conveyor. **Check the seal and the shrink** on the first packs and
   periodically: a full, continuous seal, film shrunk tight, no holes, tears, or scorching.

### 3. Watch the film and the hot parts

- If film **tears after sealing**, adjust the shrink waiting time / temperature (or get Maintenance).
- If **film builds up on the blade or in the chamber**, stop and clear it — but **only once it has
  cooled**. Film on hot parts is a fire risk.
- A **bad seal or a hole in the wrap is a reject** — set the pack aside; don't send it on.
- The film touches the baked product directly, so **film scraps or blade debris are foreign matter on the
  product.** If a piece could have gone onto a baked product, **segregate the affected packs** and tell the
  Supervisor.

### 4. Shut down

Stop the machine and switch it **off at the main switch**, then let it **cool**. Once cool, clear any
film from the sealing blade and the shrink chamber so none is left on the hot parts. **Don't reach
into the chamber or touch the blade until it's cool.**

## If something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---------|-----------|
| Weak / incomplete seal | Blade temperature too low, or product too close to the blade — adjust per the chart; if it persists, get Maintenance (blade/PTFE wear). |
| Film not shrinking tight / scorching | Adjust shrink temperature and time on the chart; check the film is the right type. |
| Film tears after sealing | Adjust the shrink waiting time; if it persists, get Maintenance. |
| Film on the blade or in the chamber | Stop; **let it cool**, then clear it. Fire risk. |
| Machine won't cycle / fault on the panel | Check the E-stop is released and guards closed; read the panel message; get Maintenance. |
| Smoke, burning smell, or flame | **E-stop, kill power at the main switch, and follow the fire procedure.** |

## Cleaning

This machine has **no dedicated cleaning SOP or record form** — it is packaging equipment with no
food-contact wet clean, and the operator confirmed none is required. Clearing film scraps off the
sealing blade and out of the shrink chamber is done **as part of operation** (sections 3–4), for fire
safety and to keep film off the baked product. If a documented housekeeping frequency is wanted for audit, add
the machine as a line on the **FRM-901 Master Sanitation Schedule**.

## Records

Machine settings go on the **batch / settings chart**. Operator training sign-off is held in the
training record. All retained per the record retention policy.

## Film is a direct food-contact material

The shrink film **touches the baked product directly**, so it is a food-contact material:

- Only the **approved food-grade film** may be used — film **certified/approved for direct food
  contact**, with the supplier's **letter of guarantee or certificate of conformance on file** (**SQF
  2.3.2.6**). Don't substitute an unapproved film.
- Because the film contacts the product, **film scraps and blade debris are a foreign-matter hazard on
  the baked product** — keep the blade and chamber clear, and hold/segregate product if a fragment could have
  reached it (**SQF 11.7.3.1**; section 3).
