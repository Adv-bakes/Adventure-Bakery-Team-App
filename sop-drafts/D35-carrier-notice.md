# Carrier and customer notice — vehicle checks before loading (D-35)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Announce the vehicle check before it starts, so nobody is refused at the dock without warning |
| **Deliverable** | D-35 — enables tasks 35.2 and 35.7; implements FSQM-036 Part 1 |
| **Sent by** | Admin |
| **Send to** | Every customer who collects, and any carrier they name |
| **Send before** | The effective date of FSQM-036 |

---

## Why this goes out first

FSQM-036 Part 1 requires that the customer or carrier be told the storage requirement and the
condition the vehicle must be in **before** a collection. This notice is how that starts, and it is
the step that makes the check workable rather than confrontational.

A collecting driver who has never been inspected will not take being turned away well, and the
person doing the turning away is whoever is on the floor. Sending this first moves the argument from
the dock to an email, weeks earlier, with the people who can actually decide something.

**Keep what you send and to whom.** SQF will want evidence that the requirement was communicated, not
just that it exists. Filed sent emails are enough — they do not need a form.

**It is also the template for the next one.** Part 1's obligation is ongoing, not a one-off: a new
customer, or an existing one who changes carrier, gets the same notice before their first collection.

---

## The notice

> **Subject: Vehicle checks before loading — from [DATE]**
>
> Dear [Customer],
>
> From **[DATE]**, we will check every vehicle before it is loaded with finished product. This
> applies to all collections, including vehicles you or your carrier arrange.
>
> **What we look at.** Before loading, we check the load space for:
>
> - cleanliness and dryness
> - freedom from odour
> - residue from a previous load
> - signs of pest activity
> - soundness and weatherproofing — holes, gaps, damaged door seals
> - anything that could damage packaging in transit
>
> It takes about a minute.
>
> **We cannot load a vehicle whose previous load was a non-food material.** If your driver is not sure
> what the vehicle last carried, please ask them to find out before arriving.
>
> **If a vehicle does not pass**, we will not load it. We will tell the driver why at the time and
> contact you straight away so a replacement can be arranged. We would far rather find a problem at
> our dock than have you find it when the product arrives.
>
> **Our product ships ambient.** No refrigeration is needed in transit — but the load space must still
> be dry and protected from weather.
>
> **What makes this quick.** A driver arriving with an empty, clean, dry load space who can say what
> the previous load was will be through the check in a minute, with nothing delayed.
>
> This is part of our food safety management system under the SQF Food Safety Code, and it protects
> the product you are collecting.
>
> If anything here conflicts with your own transport arrangements, please tell us **before [DATE]** so
> we can work it out in advance rather than at the dock.
>
> Kind regards,
>
> [Name]
> SQF Practitioner · Adventure Bakery, LLC

---

## Before you send it

1. **Set [DATE].** It should be on the far side of briefing Diana and Christina and of FRM-801 being
   available — the check needs somewhere to be recorded from the first load. Two weeks' notice is
   ample and reads as courteous rather than abrupt.
2. **Decide who signs.** SQF Practitioner is the natural authority for a food-safety requirement, and
   is what the draft says. If the customer relationship makes it better coming from the Managing
   Partner, that is a fine choice too — but then say *"on behalf of our SQF Practitioner"*, so the
   requirement is visibly owned by the role that owns the system.
3. **Consider naming a contact.** A named person to reply to gets you a conversation; a general
   address gets you silence and then a surprised driver.

## What it does not do

It does not close 11.6.5.3. **Securing the vehicle from tampering is a separate requirement**, the
site has not chosen a method, and the vacuum seal inside each box — real tamper evidence though it is
— answers a different question. That stays open as task 35.4.
