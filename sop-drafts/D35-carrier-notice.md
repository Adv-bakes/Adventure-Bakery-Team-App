# Carrier and customer notice — vehicle checks before loading (D-35)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Tell customers and their carriers that vehicles are now checked before loading |
| **Deliverable** | D-35 — discharges the Part 1 notification rule |
| **Sent by** | Admin |
| **Send to** | Every customer who collects, and any carrier they name |
| **Send when** | Now. FSQM-036 took effect 2026-09-10 and the check is already a requirement |

---

## Read this before you send it

**The timing is not what it was drafted for.** This notice was written to go out *before* the check
started, so that no driver met it as a surprise. The effective date is 2026-09-10, the notice has not
gone out, and the site accepted that trade when the date was set. So it is now a notification of
something already in force, not advance warning — and it should be sent quickly rather than polished.
The wording below is present tense for that reason.

**It is not an SQF requirement, and don't tell customers it is.** Nothing in 11.6.5 requires a carrier
to be told anything; 11.6.5.1 asks for practices that maintain the product's storage conditions and
prevent cross-contamination. An earlier version of this page claimed SQF wanted evidence of the
communication. It doesn't. FSQM-036 Part 1 requires the notice because **the site chose to**, for the
reason below — and a rule the site set itself is still a rule it has to keep.

**Why it is worth sending anyway.** A collecting driver who has never been inspected will not take
being turned away well, and the person doing the turning away is whoever is on the floor. This moves
that argument from the dock to an email, with the people who can actually decide something.

**Keep the sent copy.** FSQM-036's Records section lists it. No acknowledgement is required and none
is tracked — that is a deliberate decision, so do not build a chase-list.

**It is the template for the next one.** Part 1's obligation is not a one-off: a new customer, or an
existing one who changes carrier, gets this before their first collection.

## The notice

> **Subject: Vehicle checks before loading**
>
> Dear [Customer],
>
> We now check every vehicle before it is loaded with finished product. This applies to all
> collections, including vehicles you or your carrier arrange.
>
> **What we look at.** Before loading, we check the load space for:
>
> - cleanliness and dryness
> - freedom from odour
> - residue from a previous load
> - signs of pest activity
> - soundness and weatherproofing — holes, gaps, damaged door seals
> - anything that could damage packaging in transit
>
> It takes about a minute.
>
> **We cannot load a vehicle whose previous load was a non-food material.** If your driver is not sure
> what the vehicle last carried, please ask them to find out before arriving.
>
> **If a vehicle does not pass**, we will not load it. We will tell the driver why at the time and
> contact you straight away so a replacement can be arranged. We would far rather find a problem at
> our dock than have you find it when the product arrives.
>
> **Before your driver leaves**, we will ask them to close and secure the load compartment — locked if
> the vehicle has a lock, otherwise latched and confirmed closed. If you or your carrier require a
> seal, tell us in advance and we will apply one and record its number.
>
> **Our product ships ambient.** No refrigeration is needed in transit — but the load space must still
> be dry and protected from weather.
>
> **What makes this quick.** A driver arriving with an empty, clean, dry load space who can say what
> the previous load was will be through the check in a minute, with nothing delayed.
>
> This is part of our food safety management system under the SQF Food Safety Code, and it protects
> the product you are collecting.
>
> If anything here conflicts with your own transport arrangements, please tell us as soon as possible
> so we can work it out in advance rather than at the dock.
>
> Kind regards,
>
> [Name]
> SQF Practitioner · Adventure Bakery, LLC

---

## Two things to fill in

1. **Who signs.** SQF Practitioner is the natural authority for a food-safety requirement, and is what
   the draft says. If the customer relationship makes it better coming from the Managing Partner, that
   is a fine choice too — but then say *"on behalf of our SQF Practitioner"*, so the requirement is
   visibly owned by the role that owns the system.
2. **Name a contact.** A named person to reply to gets you a conversation; a general address gets you
   silence and then a surprised driver.
