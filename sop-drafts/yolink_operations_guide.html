System Overview

This deployment establishes a permanent, secure data-collection pipeline mapping telemetry from physical YoLink wireless environment sensors directly into a central Supabase relational table. An asynchronous background process runs under systemd supervision on a dedicated Hostinger Virtual Private Server. The captured readings power the Team Portal Temperature Monitoring report (Compliance → Temperature Logs).

The system has two halves, and this document covers both:

  1. Ingestion — the VPS daemon that receives sensor telemetry and writes it to temperature_logs. Everything up to the cheat sheet below.
  2. Alerting — a scheduled check that reads those rows every fifteen minutes, judges each in-service unit against a written limit, and emails somebody when a unit is out of range, has stopped reporting, or has a flat battery. See Automatic Alerting & Escalation.

Collecting readings is not the same as monitoring them. Until the alerting was added, a walk-in could sit warm — or a sensor could stop reporting entirely — and nothing would say so until somebody happened to open the report. In the first ten weeks of logging, transmission stopped three times, once for more than three days, and nobody noticed.

Hardware & Database Mapping

The Python application maps arriving telemetry packets from hex-encoded physical hardware IDs to human-readable names (via DEVICE_MAPPING in main.py) before validating parameters and inserting rows. Update that dictionary when a sensor is added or replaced.

Device Hardware ID Mapped Room Label Database Assignment
d88b4c010010b5da Walk-In Refrigerator public.temperature_logs
d88b4c010010b513 Walk-In Freezer public.temperature_logs
d88b4c010010b70a Bakery Floor public.temperature_logs

The mapped room label is a join key — do not rename it casually

Each name in DEVICE_MAPPING is written into temperature_logs.equipment_name, and the alerting matches limits to readings by that exact string (temperature_limits.equipment_name). There is no foreign key and no constraint tying the two together.

So if you rename a unit in main.py — or add a sensor without adding its limits row — readings keep arriving and the report keeps drawing them, but the alerting silently stops covering that unit. Nothing errors; the run simply reports fewer units checked. Change both sides together, then confirm with the query in troubleshooting item F.

2. Linux Background Daemon Integration (systemd)

Defined at /etc/systemd/system/yolink-logger.service. Restart=always revives the process after crashes or reboots.

[Unit]
Description=YoLink MQTT Temperature Logger to Supabase
After=network-online.target
Wants=network-online.target

[Service]
Type=simple
User=root
WorkingDirectory=/root/yolink-logger
ExecStart=/root/yolink-logger/venv/bin/python main.py
Restart=always
RestartSec=10

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target

Automatic Alerting & Escalation

Everything above gets readings into the database. This section is what turns those readings into monitoring: a scheduled job judges each in-service unit against a written limit and tells somebody when it fails.

Components

Component What it is
public.temperature_limits One row per sensor: the limit, whether the unit is in service, how long a silence is tolerated (stale_hours), and who gets emailed. Edited in the app — see below.
public.temperature_alerts One row per excursion, opened and closed automatically. Append-only: no client may INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE. The only writable part is the acknowledgement, through a database function.
temperature-alert Supabase Edge Function holding the logic. Deploy with supabase functions deploy temperature-alert. Needs RESEND_API_KEY in the project's Edge Function secrets to send mail.
temperature-alert-check pg_cron job on */15 * * * * that calls the function. Inspect with select * from cron.job;, history in cron.job_run_details.

What raises an alert

Kind Condition
out_of_range The two most recent readings are both beyond the unit's limit. Two rather than one, so a door held open while ingredients are carried out does not page anyone. A reading exactly at the limit passes; beyond it fails.
no_data Nothing logged for longer than the unit's stale_hours (default 6). This is the one that matters most. A dead sensor reads as perfect compliance, so an alert that fired only on a bad reading would have stayed silent through all three historical logging gaps.
low_battery The sensor reports a low battery, or level 1 of 4 — which is how no_data usually begins.

Behaviour worth knowing before you debug it

  • One open alert per unit per kind. Enforced by a partial unique index in the database rather than by the function, so a unit sitting warm for six hours produces one alert and one email — not twenty-four — and two overlapping runs cannot both open one.
  • Unacknowledged alerts are re-sent every 24 hours until somebody responds, so one missed email does not mean a warm walk-in goes unnoticed indefinitely.
  • A stale sensor is not also judged against its limit. Its last reading may be three days old; treating that as current would raise a second, misleading alert and send the responder to the wrong problem.
  • Out-of-service units and the Bakery Floor raise nothing. The Bakery Floor is ambient monitoring, not a storage unit, and has no limit by design. A unit is placed out of service when it is switched off, not when it merely happens to be empty.
  • Alerts close themselves when readings return within limits, and send a short "Resolved" email.

Configuring limits and recipients

In the Team Portal: Compliance → Temperature Monitoring → Temperature Alerts → Limits. The editor is visible to admin and owner accounts only; staff see the limits as a read-only table, deliberately — a limit is what compliance means, so changing one is not a floor-level action. Each storage unit carries its own limit, staleness tolerance and recipient list.

Leaving the recipient list blank falls back to every admin and owner account's email address. That default exists so alerting works from the moment it is deployed rather than after somebody remembers to fill in a column — but it tracks whoever currently holds an admin role, so it drifts: grant somebody admin next year for an unrelated reason and they begin receiving walk-in temperature alerts. Set recipients explicitly per unit once the system is in routine use.

Testing tip: before deliberately tripping an alert, pin the unit's recipients to a single address — and leave the pin in place until the alert has cleared. The resolution email uses whatever the list says at the moment it closes, not what it said when it opened.

Responding to an alert

Open the alert on the Temperature Monitoring page and acknowledge it with what you found and what you did. That sentence is the corrective-action record; it is written once and cannot be edited afterwards, so record the actual time and the probe reading. An alert nobody acknowledged is only a notification — the acknowledgement is what makes it evidence.

The procedure governing the response, the limits and the monthly review is SOP-401 Temperature-Controlled Storage, recorded on FRM-401 Temperature Monitoring Review. (Both are drafts at the time of writing and not yet in force; this guide describes the system they will govern.) Product whose safety is in doubt after an excursion is placed on Hold under FSQM-018 and recorded on FRM-702 — that decision belongs to the SQF Practitioner, not to whoever happens to answer the email.

Standard Troubleshooting Protocols

A. MQTT “Not authorized” / readings stop after ~2 hours

Symptom: the logs show Connected to YoLink MQTT Broker with result code Not authorized, and rows stop landing in temperature_logs — often after the service has been up for a couple of hours.

Cause: a YoLink access token is valid for only 2 hours (7200s) and is used as the MQTT username. If the client reconnects using the same cached token after it has expired, the broker rejects it as “Not authorized” on every retry.

Fix (already in main.py): the daemon proactively fetches a fresh token and rebuilds the connection every 100 minutes (TOKEN_REFRESH_SECONDS), staying under the 2-hour expiry, and refuses to “subscribe” on a failed connection. If you see this again: first restart the service (forces a new token). If it recurs immediately after a restart — not hours later — the credentials are stale; re-issue them (item B) and update .env.

  1. YoLink Credential (UAID / Secret) Problems A token response of {"state":"error","msg":"client_id not existed"} means the UAID sent was blank or unrecognized. Re-issue credentials in the YoLink app: Settings → Account → Advanced Settings → User Access Credentials (re-issuing invalidates the previous pair), then update YOLINK_UAID and YOLINK_SECRET_KEY in .env and restart. Verify a token can be obtained (run on the VPS with the values loaded): 
    curl -s -X POST https://api.yosmart.com/open/yolink/token \
  -d "grant_type=client_credentials&client_id=$YOLINK_UAID&client_secret=$YOLINK_SECRET_KEY"
    A JSON body containing access_token = credentials good; an error body = env/credential problem. Note: .env is read by the service, not your login shell — echo $YOLINK_UAID in a plain terminal looks empty even when the service is configured correctly.
  2. Database Authentication Failures (401 Errors) Verify that the secret service_role API key remains populated inside .env (SUPABASE_KEY). The anon key is blocked by Row-Level-Security and will fail every insert; it is never safe to expose.
  3. Sensors Not Updating YoLink equipment transmits selectively on temperature change or on a 30–60 min heartbeat to conserve battery, so gaps are normal. Press the physical SET button on the sensor to force an instant broadcast. If one sensor is persistently silent, check its battery gauge on the Temperature Monitoring report — a low battery (level 1) can stop transmissions.
    Since alerting was added: short gaps of 30–60 min remain normal and raise nothing, but a silence longer than the unit's stale_hours (default 6) now opens a no_data alert and emails the recipients. A no_data alert means the unit is unmonitored, not that it is warm — take manual probe readings until logging resumes.
  4. Verifying Raw Transmission Packets Stop the daemon and run the script by hand inside the virtualenv to watch raw JSON hit the pipeline live; restart the service when finished: 
    sudo systemctl stop yolink-logger.service
cd /root/yolink-logger && source venv/bin/activate && python main.py
# Ctrl-C when done, then:
sudo systemctl start yolink-logger.service
  5. No alert emails arriving when readings are clearly out of limits. Work down this list in order — each step tells you which half failed. 
    -- (a) did the job run at all?
select status, start_time, return_message from cron.job_run_details d
  join cron.job j using (jobid)
 where j.jobname = 'temperature-alert-check'
 order by start_time desc limit 5;

-- (b) did it open an alert?
select equipment_name, kind, opened_at, worst_value, cleared_at, notify_count
  from public.temperature_alerts order by opened_at desc limit 5;

-- (c) did it try to send, and what happened?
select status, recipient_email, error_message, created_at
  from public.email_send_log
 where template_name = 'temperature-alert' order by created_at desc limit 5;

-- (d) is the unit even eligible? only kind='storage' AND in_service are checked
select equipment_name, kind, in_service, min_f, max_f, stale_hours, notify_emails
  from public.temperature_limits order by equipment_name;
    A row in (c) reading not_configured means either RESEND_API_KEY is missing from the Edge Function secrets, or the unit has no recipients and no admin/owner account has an email on file. If (d) shows the unit missing entirely, see the join-key warning under Hardware & Database Mapping. The function's own response reports checked and skipped counts, which is the quickest way to confirm coverage.
  6. An alert is stuck open Alerts close on their own once readings are back inside the limit — confirm the unit is genuinely within range and that readings are still arriving. A no_data alert necessarily stays open while the sensor is silent, because a sensor that is not reporting cannot demonstrate recovery. Alerts are never closed by editing the table: it has no UPDATE policy at all, and acknowledging an alert is not the same as closing it.

First-Response Quick Guide

If you see... Do this first
Report data looks stale / no recent readings systemctl status, then restart the service; watch journalctl -f
Logs say “Not authorized” Restart service (new token). If instant on restart, re-issue YoLink credentials.
Logs say insert / 401 / RLS error Check SUPABASE_KEY is the service-role key in .env
One sensor missing, others fine Press SET on that sensor; check its battery level on the report
After a server reboot Service auto-starts (Restart=always); confirm with systemctl status
A no_data alert email arrived The unit is unmonitored, not necessarily warm. Probe it by hand and record the reading, then fix the sensor or the daemon
An out_of_range alert arrived Check the door and airflow, probe the unit, correct the cause, then acknowledge the alert with what you did — that is the record
Out of limits, but no email came Troubleshooting item F: cron ran? alert opened? email_send_log? unit in service?
Alerts for a unit stopped entirely Check its temperature_limits row still exists and its equipment_name matches DEVICE_MAPPING exactly
